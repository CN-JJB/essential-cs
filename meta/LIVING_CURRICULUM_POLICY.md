# Living Curriculum Policy

## Time sensitivity

Classify relevant content as:

- **STABLE** — durable principles/models;
- **CURRENT** — current engineering practice/tool reality;
- **FRONTIER** — fast-moving material included only when pedagogically justified.

Suggested review cadence:

- STABLE: 18–24 months or after a relevant major change;
- CURRENT: 6–12 months;
- FRONTIER: 3–6 months;
- Labs: continuous CI/smoke testing plus periodic human/AI review.

## Technology Admission Test

Before adding modern technology, ask:

1. What problem does it solve?
2. What stable principle is behind it?
3. What trade-off does it make?
4. Is adoption sufficiently real/sustained for this teaching role?
5. If the product dies, is the lesson still useful?
6. Is a classic/simpler technology pedagogically better?

Possible outcomes:

- CORE
- CURRENT CASE
- FRONTIER
- DEEP DIVE
- REJECT

## Lifecycle

Technology/material may transition:

`ACTIVE → LEGACY → HISTORICAL → RETIRED`

A living curriculum must intentionally forget. Retain old technology only for current or historical pedagogical value.

## Maintenance queue

Track:

- overdue CURRENT reviews;
- broken labs;
- changed source/spec behavior;
- stale screenshots/visuals where materially misleading;
- FRONTIER reassessment;
- dependency/environment drift;
- Errata/critical content bugs.
