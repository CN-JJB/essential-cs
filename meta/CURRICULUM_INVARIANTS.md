# Curriculum Invariants

These principles define the identity of Essential CS. Changing them requires a major RFC and an explicit Decision Record.

1. **Modern computing-system judgment is the highest goal.** Coverage and implementation exist to improve Trace, Explain, Observe, Diagnose, Correctness, Judge, Estimate, and Learn-New-Tech capabilities.
2. **Accessible, not dumbed-down.** Reduce accidental cognitive load, not technical truth.
3. **Hands-on exists to create understanding.** Do not maximize implementation for its own sake.
4. **Principle before product.** Products and frameworks are cases through which stable ideas and trade-offs are examined.
5. **Real mechanism over fake simulation.** When a mechanism can be safely and clearly observed in a real system, prefer that.
6. **AI is not an authority.** AI may research, draft, synthesize, maintain, review, and create visuals, but technical claims require evidence.
7. **Evidence and verification outrank fluent prose.**
8. **Complexity must justify itself.** Every added abstraction, dependency, component, and course topic must explain what it buys and where complexity moves.
9. **Core preserves a complete shared world model.** Deep Dives may specialize only after the learner can connect the main modern computing chain.
10. **Modern does not mean trendy.** New technology must pass a Technology Admission Test and retain pedagogical value beyond product fashion.
11. **Teach once, revisit many times.** Canonical definitions/mechanisms should have one primary teaching home; later contexts revisit through application, connection, failure, and trade-offs.
12. **GitHub is the project memory.** Important project state, decisions, evidence, and handoffs must be persisted in the repository.
13. **Long curriculum, short feedback loops.** No global time ceiling; learner-visible stages must provide capability gains, checkpoints, project milestones, and exit criteria.
14. **Self-study first.** Material must support independent learners without requiring a lecturer to repair missing explanations.
15. **Open-first and vendor-neutral.** Core principles/labs should not depend on a commercial vendor; proprietary systems may appear as reality-aware case studies.
16. **Correctness, failure, measurement, security, and cost are horizontal concerns.** They must recur throughout the curriculum rather than be isolated as late add-ons.
17. **Research before important writing.** Important modules require a Research Dossier before large-scale lesson drafting.
18. **Classic, proven experiments before custom demos.** Adopt → Adapt → Build.
19. **No major curriculum decisions while writing a lesson.** Escalate to Open Question / RFC / Decision.
20. **v1.0 means teachable, not merely written.** A stable release requires runnable Core labs, multi-role verification, learner validation, an external coverage audit, and an operating maintenance system.
