# Research & Source Policy

## Principle

AI may search, summarize, synthesize, draft, and maintain content. **AI is never the factual authority.**

Important modules require a Research Dossier before large-scale lesson writing.

## Evidence priority

Prefer, as appropriate:

1. standards, specifications, RFCs;
2. official documentation;
3. classic textbooks;
4. classic papers;
5. high-quality university courses;
6. high-quality maintained source code;
7. mature engineering documentation and official postmortems.

The course must remain self-contained. External sources constrain and deepen teaching; they do not replace it with a link list.

## Research Dossier

Before an important module is drafted, record:

- learning goal and target mental model;
- canonical sources;
- classic textbooks/courses/papers/specs/docs;
- candidate classic labs and teaching projects;
- Adopt / Adapt / Build analysis;
- candidate Source Expeditions;
- modern reality classified as STABLE / CURRENT / FRONTIER;
- common misconceptions;
- open questions;
- provenance/licensing risks.

## Evidence layers

Distinguish:

- PRINCIPLE
- SPECIFICATION
- IMPLEMENTATION
- CURRENT PRACTICE

When sources appear to conflict, first determine whether they describe different layers, versions, environments, or constraints.

True disagreements should be represented as disagreements/trade-offs rather than flattened.

Confidence/context labels may include:

- ESTABLISHED
- IMPLEMENTATION-SPECIFIC
- CURRENT-PRACTICE
- CONTESTED
- UNCERTAIN

Evidence-insufficient claims must be marked uncertain. Do not fill missing details with plausible speculation.

## Provenance

Important Module/Lesson/Lab work should preserve lightweight provenance for:

- principle;
- specification;
- implementation claim;
- current practice;
- experiment inspiration;
- visual source.

For adapted labs/materials, record origin, retained parts, changes/deletions, and license.

## Modern content

Modern/current claims must be rechecked against current primary or authoritative sources during review. Product popularity alone is not an admission criterion.
